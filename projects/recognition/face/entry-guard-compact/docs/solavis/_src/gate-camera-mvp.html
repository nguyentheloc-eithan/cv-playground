Gate Camera MVP

You sell a compact solar 4G camera made from off-the-shelf parts. Customers pay a subscription. All faces, photos and settings live in your cloud, so you decide what each customer can do, and you can switch a camera off remotely.

The idea in one paragraph

The camera is deliberately simple: it wakes when someone walks past, takes a photo, finds the face, and sends the small face crop to your server. Your server recognises the person, stores the event, and alerts the customer. The camera holds no face data and no storage keys. Because everything important sits on your server, one camera design serves many customers, and plans, limits and cut-offs are database settings that you can change at any time without touching the hardware.

The camera talks to one address only: your API. It has no cloud storage password. To upload a photo it asks your API for a short-lived upload link, and your API refuses the request if the customer's plan is off or over its limit.

What you sell

The camera

One sealed unit with a small solar panel, one battery, a motion sensor, a white light, a camera and (in the 4G version) a SIM slot. Same hardware for every customer.

The service

Customer web app (works on a phone, no app store needed): register people, mark banned people, see events, manage cameras, see plan and usage.

The control

You hold the accounts, data, plans and device keys. A customer who stops paying loses service, and the camera becomes a paperweight until reactivated.

Power check

The camera sleeps most of the day. I estimate about 1 Wh/day including 4G bursts. A 1.5 W panel gives about 3 Wh/day in good light and about 1.7 Wh/day in a Hà Nội winter. One 18650 holds about 11 Wh, so roughly 10 days with no sun. Measure this on the bench in week 1 before you trust it.

How a customer's status is enforced

  1. You register each camera in an inventory table when you build it, with its ID, secret and a printed QR claim code.
  2. The customer scans the QR in the web app, creates an account and claims the camera. A 14-day trial starts.
  3. On every wake-up the camera asks the API for its config. The answer includes the plan state: active trial past due suspended. If suspended, it uploads nothing and sleeps for 6 hours.
  4. The API also checks the limits on every upload, so modified firmware cannot get around them.
  5. Past due gives 7 days of grace, then suspended. Data is kept 30 days, then deleted.

Payments for the MVP: prepaid periods (1, 6, 12 months) paid by bank transfer with a VietQR code, and you switch the account on by hand. Automatic payment (PayOS, VNPay or MoMo, or Stripe for overseas) comes after you have paying users.

Camera security and set-up

  • Unique key per camera. Never one shared password. A stolen or sold-on camera is blocked by revoking its key.
  • Factory step, about 3 minutes per camera: a script flashes the firmware, creates the ID and key, writes them to the board, adds the camera to your inventory, and prints the QR sticker.
  • Customer Wi-Fi set-up: the camera opens a temporary hotspot, GateCam-XXXX. The customer joins it on their phone and picks their Wi-Fi.
  • Updates over the air: signed firmware, rolled out to a few cameras first, with automatic rollback if the new version fails to start.
  • Later, before volume: turn on ESP32 secure boot and flash encryption. Both are one-way, so test them on spare boards first.

Per customer, per month

Wi-Fi customer

Storage and compute are close to nothing: 100 events a day at 40 KB for 30 days is about 120 MB. Expect under 10 k ₫ per month including a share of the server.

4G customer

Add the SIM. A backup-only SIM uses little data, but plan for 50–100 k ₫ a month and decide whether the plan price covers it or the customer buys their own SIM.

Example margin, using my sample numbers: sell the 4G camera at about 2.9 M ₫ against a 1.9 M ₫ cost, then 149 k ₫ a month on Plus against roughly 100 k ₫ of SIM and server. That is thin if you include the SIM, so the SIM policy is the biggest pricing choice. Set real prices after the pilot.

Roadmap to 10 weeks

  1. Week 1bench

    Buy 2 kits and prove the camera

    Test wake from PIR, face crop quality with the OV2640 at 2 m, 4G upload time, and real power draw.

    Pass if: wake to upload under 8 s on 4G, under 1.5 Wh/day, and recognition correct on at least 9 of 10 test people.

  2. Weeks 2–5software

    Firmware and multi-tenant backend

    Firmware for wake, capture, upload, config, hotspot set-up and over-the-air updates. Backend with tenants, devices, plans, recognition worker and admin console.

    Pass if: two test customers cannot see each other's data, and suspending one stops its camera within one wake-up.

  3. Weeks 5–7customer app

    Customer web app and alerts

    Claim a camera by QR, register people with consent, banned list, events feed, Telegram alerts, plan and usage page.

  4. Weeks 7–8build

    Factory script and 5 units

    Provisioning script with QR labels, final enclosure print, assemble 5 cameras.

    Pass if: one camera takes under 30 minutes from parts to labelled unit.

  5. Weeks 9–10pilot

    5 friendly users, manual billing

    Install at their gates, charge by bank transfer, watch every alert, log resets and battery voltage.

    Pass if: no dead battery, at most 1 false banned alert per week per camera, and at least 3 of 5 users agree to keep paying.

After the pilot: automatic payments, a proper plan editor, a first custom PCB to cut cost and size, radio type approval, and secure boot.

Risks you carry as the seller

RiskWhat to do
Personal data lawFace data is sensitive personal data. You process the faces of your customers' visitors. Vietnam's Decree 13/2023 and the newer personal data law set rules on consent, notice, and an impact assessment filing. Get a lawyer to check your terms and filings before public sale.
Banned listsCustomers add the names. Your terms must say they own the list, must not use police or third-party criminal data, must show a notice at the gate, and must review a match before acting. Alerts say "possible match".
Radio approvalSelling 4G and Wi-Fi devices needs Vietnamese type approval for the finished product. Certified boards help, but do not replace it. Pilot with friendly users first.
Battery shippingLithium cells have shipping limits, especially by air. Use protected cells and ground transport.
AccuracyA cheap camera struggles with backlight and night. Tune thresholds on pilot data, and never use the camera to unlock a door.
Heat and rainLight-coloured ASA, a sun hood, silicone seals and a vent plug. Log battery voltage and board temperature from the first day.
