Solar Gate Camera

A small solar-powered camera at your gate. It recognises people on the device, flags banned people, uploads event snapshots to your cloud, and switches from Wi-Fi to a 4G SIM when Wi-Fi drops. Every part can be bought in Vietnam.

Recommended build

  • Brain: Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W running your existing Python recognition code, headless, with no browser needed.
  • Eyes: Pi Camera Module 3 (autofocus) plus a small white LED that switches on when someone approaches at night.
  • Network: Wi-Fi first. A 4G LTE USB dongle or SIM7600 HAT is the backup. Only small snapshots go over 4G.
  • Power: 40 W solar panel, MPPT charge controller, 12.8 V 12 Ah LiFePO4 battery (about 3 days without sun).
  • Cloud: any S3-compatible bucket (Cloudflare R2, Backblaze B2, AWS S3), plus Telegram for instant alerts.

How it works

Recognition runs on the device, so the camera still works and still alerts locally when the internet is down. The cloud only receives results: a small snapshot, a name or "unknown", a score, and a timestamp. Events are queued on the SD card and sent when a link comes back.

  AG -->|face vector| REC[recognize.py check]
  REC --> DB[(SQLite: people, vectors, outbox)]

  NET -->|primary| WIFI[Wi-Fi]

Panel

40 W × 4 sun hours × 0.75 ≈ 120 Wh/day in the south and centre. A Hà Nội winter gives about 2 sun hours, so ≈ 60 Wh/day, which is just enough. A 20 W panel is not enough.

Battery

154 Wh ÷ 55 Wh ≈ 2.8 days with no sun. Do not discharge below 20%, so plan on about 2 days of real reserve.

These are design estimates. Phase 0 measures the real draw with a USB power meter before any panel or battery is bought.

Wi-Fi to 4G failover

  • Use NetworkManager. Give Wi-Fi the lower route metric (preferred) and the 4G interface the higher one, so Linux switches on its own when Wi-Fi goes down.
  • "Wi-Fi connected" is not "internet works". A small link monitor pings your bucket endpoint every 30 s. After 3 failures it brings up 4G, and it checks Wi-Fi again every 5 minutes to switch back.
  • Save SIM data: on 4G send only 640 px JPEG snapshots (≈ 40 KB) and JSON events. Video clips and full-size images wait for Wi-Fi.
  • If both links fail, the agent keeps recognising and queues events in SQLite. A local buzzer or relay output can still trigger on a banned match.
  • Use a data SIM from Viettel, VinaPhone or MobiFone with a monthly data plan. Disable voice and SMS-based extras.

Keep faces.py, db.py and recognize.py. The security console stays as the office tool for registering people. The device gets a new headless agent.

Verdict rules at the gate

  • Known Logged only. Optional silent Telegram message for family or staff arrivals.
  • Unknown Snapshot to cloud, normal notification.
  • Banned match Immediate alert with snapshot and score. Require two matches in a row above a high threshold before alerting, to cut false alarms.

Cloud, alerts and data

Storage

Any S3-compatible bucket works through one library. Cloudflare R2 and Backblaze B2 have free tiers and cheap storage. Layout: /gate-01/2026/11/23/14-05-33_unknown.jpg plus a matching .json. Add a lifecycle rule that deletes snapshots after 30–90 days.

Alerts

Telegram bot first: free, instant, works in Vietnam, and supports photos. Zalo OA can be added later if family members do not use Telegram.

Security

Use a bucket key that can only write to one prefix. Encrypt the disk or at least the face vectors. Keep tokens in environment variables and rotate them if a device is stolen.

Cheapest option: custom ESP32-S3 board

A custom PCB around an ESP32-S3 module cuts the unit cost from about 6.3 M ₫ to about 1.1 M ₫ (0.75 M ₫ without 4G). It sleeps until a PIR sensor fires, so it uses roughly 1 Wh/day instead of 55. One 18650 pair and a 5 W panel are enough. The price is that the small chip cannot run your current Python recognition, so the heavy part moves to a cheap server.

How it runs

  • Deep sleep. The PIR wakes the chip, the camera takes 3–5 frames, on-device face detection keeps the best crop (10–20 KB).
  • It tries Wi-Fi for 5 s. If that fails it powers the 4G module, uploads the crop, then powers the module off again.
  • The server runs your recognize.py on the crop and sends the Telegram alert and the cloud log. The server also holds the people list and the banned list, so the device stores no face data.
  • A person who walks past fast may be partly missed in the first second of wake-up. The PIR sees about 5–6 m ahead, which gives the board lead time. Test this early.

PCB notes for Altium

  • Use a certified ESP32-S3 module and keep the antenna end clear of copper and the battery. Do not design the RF part from the bare chip.
  • The 4G module pulls peaks of about 2 A. Place 470 µF or more of bulk capacitance next to it, use wide traces, and switch it with a load switch so it is fully off between events.
  • Charge Li-ion only between 0 and 45 °C. Put an NTC on the cell and keep the battery in the shaded part of the box, since Vietnamese heat can exceed that inside a closed enclosure.
  • Add test pads, a boot and reset button, a debug UART header, and a solar input fuse plus TVS diode.
  • Export Gerber, BOM and pick-and-place files, and choose LCSC "basic" parts where you can to keep assembly fees down. Altium is paid software, so if you only have a trial, KiCad produces the same fab files.
  • Plan two board spins. The first one usually has at least one mistake.

Selling this as a product needs radio type approval in Vietnam for the 4G and Wi-Fi parts. A certified module helps but does not replace the approval for the finished device. All figures here are my estimates, so confirm them with a first prototype on a breadboard before ordering boards.

ESP32 build: plan and cost to a 5-unit pilot

About 12 weeks and ≈ 17 M ₫, not counting your own time. Most of the money is two rounds of assembled boards, because the first round almost always has a mistake.

  1. Phase 1weeks 1–3

    Breadboard prototype

    ESP32-S3 camera dev board, a 4G dev board (A7670C), PIR, charger and panel. Write the firmware: sleep, PIR wake, face crop, upload, power off 4G. Build the small server that runs recognize.py and sends Telegram.

    Pass if: wake to alert in under 8 s on 4G, average draw under 1.5 Wh/day, and a walking person is caught at 3 m.

  2. Phase 2weeks 3–5

    Schematic and PCB v1 in Altium

    Design from the proven prototype, order 5 assembled boards from JLCPCB or PCBWay (about 5 days to build, 5–10 days shipping).

    Pass if: design review done against the checklist (power, RF keepout, 4G peak current, battery temperature cutoff).

  3. Phase 3weeks 6–7

    Bring-up and fixes

    Measure real sleep current and 4G peaks, test solar charging in the sun, list every board error.

    Pass if: sleep current under 200 µA and the board runs 3 days from the battery with no sun.

  4. Phase 4weeks 8–9

    PCB v2 and enclosures

    Fix the errors, order 5 more boards, fit them in IP65 boxes with the panel on a pole bracket.

  5. Phase 5weeks 10–12

    Field pilot, 3 weeks

    Run at your gate and two other sites. Review every alert, tune thresholds, log battery voltage and CPU resets.

    Pass if: no dead battery, at most 1 false banned alert per week, and missed arrivals under 5%.

One-box enclosure for 3D printing

Everything sits in one wedge-shaped box: the solar panel on the sloped top, the camera, motion sensor and LED on the front, and the board and batteries inside. You screw the back plate to the gate post or wall, then the cover slides on and is held by 4 screws from underneath. The wall screws are hidden under the cover, which helps against theft. This is Rev A: it has not been printed or fitted yet.

Size

200 wide × 150 deep × 120 high (80 at the front), in mm. The size is set by the panel, which needs a pocket about 165 × 140 mm.

Front face

16 mm camera opening with a 20 mm glass or acrylic window disc, a glued-on sun hood, a 12 mm PIR opening on the left and an 8 mm LED opening on the right.

Inside

Four PCB posts on the front wall, a cradle for a 2 × 18650 holder on the floor, a cable hole under the panel, and a 12 mm hole for an optional vent plug.

  • Material: ASA (best for sun and rain). PETG is the fallback. Do not use PLA, it softens in a hot box.
  • Settings: 0.2 mm layers, at least 4 perimeters, 30% infill, black or light grey (light grey runs cooler in the sun). Ask for the outside to stay smooth, with no ironing or brim marks on the front.
  • Files and source: in the repo at hardware/enclosure/. build_enclosure.py rebuilds all three STL files after you change a size.
  • Hardware to buy with it: 4 × M3 × 12 screws, 4 × M3 heat-set inserts, 4 × M2.5 self-tapping screws for the board, 4 × wall screws with plugs, 2 mm foam tape or a silicone bead for the seam, neutral-cure silicone for the window, PIR and panel cable, and a 20 mm window disc.

Assembly order

  1. Press the 4 inserts into the shelves on the back plate with a soldering iron.
  2. Screw the back plate to the post or wall. Face the panel south, with no shade.
  3. Glue the window disc into its recess and the hood around it. Fit the PIR and LED with silicone.
  4. Glue the panel into its pocket and pass its cable through the hole. Seal the hole with silicone.
  5. Screw the board onto its posts, set the battery holder in its cradle, and connect everything.
  6. Put foam tape around the cover rim, slide the cover onto the plate, and fit the 4 screws from underneath.

Cost of the box, and what is not checked yet

The three parts use about 330 g of ASA. At 2,000–4,000 ₫ per gram that is roughly 0.7–1.3 M ₫ for a one-off print, well above the 80 k ₫ I put in the parts table. Rates differ a lot, so get quotes from two or three services in Vietnam. Before you order, check three sizes against the real parts: the solar panel (the pocket is a guess), the 18650 holder, and the board hole pattern, which is a placeholder until the Altium board exists. The body is closed with no moving parts, so for the first print I suggest only the cover in cheap PETG to test the fit.

Roadmap

  1. Phase 0week 1

    Prove the code runs on the Pi

    Buy a Pi Zero 2 W, camera and USB power meter. Run the current check on it and measure time per face and watts.

    Pass if: under 1 s per face and under 2 W average. Otherwise move to Pi 4 or add a lighter detector and revisit the power plan.

  2. Phase 1weeks 2–3

    Edge agent on the bench

    Write agent.py, uplink.py and the Telegram alert. Power from the wall, Wi-Fi only.

    Pass if: 20 registered people and 5 test "banned" faces give no missed banned match at 2 m, and the outbox survives a pulled network cable.

  3. Phase 2week 4

    4G failover

    Add the modem and netwatch.py.

    Pass if: switching to 4G takes under 60 s when Wi-Fi is switched off, and returns to Wi-Fi on its own.

  4. Phase 3week 5

    Solar and enclosure

    Panel, MPPT, LiFePO4 battery, buck converter, IP65 box. Mount at the gate at 1.5–1.7 m height, facing the entry path, with the panel on the same pole facing south.

    Pass if: 7 days outdoors with no reboot, battery never below 30%, and enclosure air temperature below 60 °C at midday.

  5. Phase 4week 6+

    Field pilot

    Run for 2 weeks at your real gate. Review every alert in the console, tune thresholds, and record false alarms per day.

    Pass if: at most 1 false banned alert per week and no missed test enrolment.

Legal note for Vietnam

Face data counts as sensitive personal data under Decree 13/2023/NĐ-CP. Put up a visible sign at the gate, get consent from family and staff you register, and keep only the retention you need. Build the banned list from your own incidents, not from police or third-party data. Check the details with a local lawyer before you sell this as a product.

Decisions I need from you

  • Does the gate have any mains power nearby? If yes, solar can become a backup and the cost drops by about 2.2 M ₫.
  • Is this for one gate or a product you will sell? A product needs a custom enclosure, a provisioning flow and a fleet dashboard, which adds about 4 more weeks.
  • Do alerts only go to you, or to several people or a guard post?
  • Which cloud do you already have an account with? That sets the first uploader.
