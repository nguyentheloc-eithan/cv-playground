1. Executive summary

Solavis is a small camera that needs no mains power and no cable. A solar panel and one battery keep it running at a gate. When someone approaches, it takes a photo, finds the face and sends a small crop to our cloud. Our server recognises the person, records the event, and alerts the owner on a phone. If Wi-Fi fails, the camera switches to a 4G SIM.

The first product is built entirely from ready-made parts, with no custom circuit board, so we can reach paying pilot users in about 10 weeks. Customers buy the camera and pay a monthly subscription. Everything that matters stays in our cloud: faces, photos, plans and device keys. That gives us control over each customer's data and service, and lets us change plan limits without touching the hardware.

Security activation and tamper protection

A new camera is unarmed. The owner claims it, mounts it, and presses Arm. From then on, if the camera is moved, turned, covered, opened or removed, it takes a photo, alerts the owner, and locks itself until the owner unlocks it with a second check. The owner can also open a timed service window, mark a camera stolen, or release it to a new owner. This adds about 0.1 M ₫ of sensors to each camera.

5. System architecture

flowchart LR
  CAM[Solavis camera] -->|HTTPS, device key| API[API: auth + plan check]
  API --> Q[Queue]
  Q --> REC[Recognition worker]
  REC --> DB[(Postgres + pgvector)]
  REC --> OBJ[(Object storage: photos)]
  REC --> NOTE[Alerts: app push, Telegram]
  APP[Customer web app] <--> API
  ADM[Admin console] <--> API
  API -->|config + plan status| CAM

Design principles

  • The camera is untrusted. It holds an ID and a key, nothing else of value. The server checks every request.
  • The server decides. Plans, thresholds and features come from the server on each wake-up.
  • Sleep first. Every design choice protects battery life.
  • Reuse what exists. The recognition code in this repository (faces.py, recognize.py, db.py) becomes the worker, changed to search one customer's people only.

6.3 Enclosure (Rev B)

A wedge-shaped box, 125 wide × 90 deep × 79 high mm (55 mm at the front). The solar panel sits in a pocket on the 15° sloped top. The camera, motion sensor and light are on the front, with a glued sun hood over the lens. The back plate screws to the post or wall, and the cover is held by 3 screws from underneath. The wall screws sit under the cover.

7. Firmware specification

Language: C++ with the Arduino or ESP-IDF framework, built with PlatformIO. Updates over the air, signed, with rollback.

7.1 Behaviour of one wake-up

  1. Wake from deep sleep on the PIR signal or the 6-hour timer.
  2. Capture 3 to 5 frames and keep the sharpest. Turn on the white light if it is dark.
  3. Detect a face on the device. If none, return to sleep after a cooldown.
  4. Connect to Wi-Fi for up to 5 seconds. If it fails and the plan allows 4G, power the modem and connect.
  5. Config: ask the API for the plan state and settings. Use the cached copy if less than 6 hours old.
  6. Upload: request a short-lived upload link, send the crop (10 to 20 KB) and the event details.
  7. Sleep. Power off the modem first.

7.2 Rules

SituationBehaviour
Plan suspendedUpload nothing. Sleep 6 hours, then ask again.
No networkKeep up to 20 crops in flash. Send when a link returns. Drop the oldest first.
Battery under 3.4 VReduce events, no night light.
Battery under 3.2 VSleep only, one heartbeat a day.
Heartbeat, every 6 hoursSend battery voltage, board temperature, signal strength, firmware version.
New firmware fails to startRoll back to the previous version automatically.

7.3 Set-up

A new camera opens a hotspot named Solavis-XXXX. The customer joins it on a phone, picks their Wi-Fi, and scans the QR sticker to claim the camera.

9. Customer app and alerts

Solavis app (web, MVP)

Installs on a phone from the browser. Claim a camera, register people with consent, set the banned list, view events, review "possible match" alerts, see plan and usage. Push notifications.

Telegram

A bot that sends the photo and verdict. The customer links it once with a code from the app. Free and instant.

Later

Zalo notifications (per-message cost), a native phone app if iPhone push proves unreliable, webhooks for Pro.

Verdicts shown to the customer

  • Known Logged. Optional quiet notice.
  • Unknown Photo and a normal alert.
  • Possible banned match Immediate alert with the photo, the stored photo and the score. It needs two matches above a high threshold, and the owner reviews it. The wording is always "possible match".

10. Data governance and security

ControlHow
Nothing sensitive on the cameraOnly its ID, key and Wi-Fi password. No faces, no storage keys.
Customer separationtenant_id on every row with Postgres row-level security. Photo paths start with t/{tenant}/d/{device}/.
EncryptionTLS in transit. Disk encryption at rest. Face vectors encrypted with a per-customer key.
Access to photosOur staff must give a reason. Every view is written to an audit log.
RetentionA nightly job removes photos older than the plan allows.
Customer rightsExport and delete on request, from the app and the admin console.
BackupsDaily, encrypted, to a second bucket. Deleted data leaves backups after 35 days.
Camera identityA unique key per camera. A stolen or resold camera is blocked by revoking the key. Uploads go through short-lived links.
FirmwareSigned updates. Secure boot and flash encryption are enabled before volume, after testing on spare boards because they cannot be undone.

11. Subscriptions and entitlements

Limits are rows in a plans table. The camera never contains them.

StateCamera behaviourCustomer sees
Trial 14 daysWorks with Basic limitsDays left, upgrade prompt
ActiveWorks with plan limitsUsage against limits
Past due 7 daysWorks, no new peoplePayment reminder
SuspendedUploads nothing, sleeps 6 h between checksHistory read-only, reactivate button
Closed after 30 daysKey revokedData deleted, with a final export offered first
  1. We register each camera in inventory with its ID, key, version and a claim code printed as a QR.
  2. The customer scans the QR, creates an account and claims the camera. The trial starts.
  3. On every wake-up the camera receives the plan state. The server also checks every upload against the limits, so modified firmware gains nothing.

Payment, MVP: prepaid periods (1, 6, 12 months) by bank transfer with a VietQR code. We activate the account by hand. Next: PayOS, VNPay or MoMo for automatic renewal, and Stripe for overseas customers.

14. Roadmap and milestones

  1. Week 1bench

    M1. Hardware proof

    Buy two kits. Test wake, crop quality, 4G upload and real power draw.

    Exit: wake to upload under 8 s on 4G, under 1.5 Wh/day, 9 of 10 test people recognised.

  2. Weeks 2–5software

    M2. Firmware and backend

    Firmware for the wake-up flow, hotspot set-up and updates. Multi-tenant API, recognition worker, admin console.

    Exit: tenant isolation and plan enforcement tests pass.

  3. Weeks 5–7app

    M3. Customer app and alerts

    Claim by QR, people with consent, banned list, events, Telegram, plan page.

  4. Weeks 7–8build

    M4. Five units

    Provisioning script, labels, final enclosure print, assembly and end-of-line test.

    Exit: under 30 minutes per unit, all five pass.

  5. Weeks 9–10pilot

    M5. Pilot with five users

    Install at their gates, bill by bank transfer, review every alert.

    Exit: no dead battery, at most 1 false banned alert per camera per week, 3 of 5 users agree to keep paying.

  6. Aftergrowth

    M6. Scale preparation

    Automatic payments, plan editor, legal filings and radio type approval, secure boot, then a first custom circuit board to cut cost and size.
