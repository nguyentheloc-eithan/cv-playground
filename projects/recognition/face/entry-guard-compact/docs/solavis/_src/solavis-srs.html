1. Introduction

1.1 Purpose

This document states what the Solavis system must do. It is the single source of requirements for the camera firmware, the cloud service, the customer app and the admin console. The hardware design (SOL-HW-001) and the software design (SOL-SW-001) both trace back to the requirement IDs here.

1.2 Scope

Solavis is planned as two product lines that share one cloud service:

  • Solavis Camera (first, the MVP): a solar-powered camera that detects a person at a gate, recognises the face in the cloud, and alerts the owner. Sold as a camera plus a monthly subscription.
  • Solavis Platform (working name, later): the same software sold on its own. Customers connect cameras they already own, set up streaming, and use our recognition, alerts, plans and apps.

This SRS specifies the Camera MVP in full. It also states the architecture requirements that the MVP must follow now (ARCH) so the Platform product does not need a rewrite, and it lists the Platform requirements (BYO) for planning. It does not cover the custom circuit board, which is a later project.

1.3 Conventions

  • Words: shall is a requirement. should is a goal that can slip. may is optional.
  • Priority: M must have for the MVP. S should have for the MVP. L later.
  • Verification: T test, D demonstration, I inspection, A analysis.
  • Numbers marked target are goals to be confirmed by measurement. Numbers marked TBD have no value yet.

2.3 Operating environment

  • Outdoors in Vietnam: heat, humidity, heavy rain, direct sun, dust.
  • Home or business Wi-Fi of varying quality, and Vietnamese 4G LTE networks (Viettel, VinaPhone, MobiFone).
  • Cloud: one Linux server for the MVP, S3-compatible storage, Postgres.
  • Phones: current Android and iOS browsers that support installable web apps and push.

2.4 Constraints

  • The MVP uses ready-made boards and parts. No custom circuit board.
  • Power is a 1.5 W solar panel and one 18650 cell, so the camera must sleep most of the time.
  • The camera cannot run the full recognition model. Recognition runs in the cloud.
  • The recognition code reuses the existing repository code, which produces 128-number face vectors.

2.5 Assumptions and dependencies

  • The selected 4G board has a camera connector, a SIM slot and a usable deep-sleep mode. To be confirmed on the real board.
  • Customers have Wi-Fi at the gate, or accept 4G with a SIM.
  • A third-party cloud (storage and Telegram) stays available and affordable.
  • Personal data law requirements are confirmed by counsel before public sale.

3.7 Secure activation and tamper protection (TAM)

A camera at a gate can be turned, covered, carried to another corner or stolen. The camera therefore ships unarmed, is armed by its owner at the final position, and then raises an alert and locks itself when anything suggests it was touched. Detection is layered, because no single sensor catches every case.

stateDiagram-v2
  [*] --> Unclaimed
  Unclaimed --> Mounting: owner claims camera
  Mounting --> Armed: owner presses Arm at final position
  Armed --> Service: owner opens a service window
  Service --> Armed: window ends or owner ends it
  Armed --> Locked: tamper detected
  Locked --> Mounting: owner unlocks with step-up check
  Armed --> Revoked: owner marks stolen
  Locked --> Revoked: owner marks stolen
  Armed --> Unclaimed: owner releases camera

Arming

IDRequirementPriVer
TAM-001A new camera shall be unarmed. It shall arm only after the owner has claimed it and pressed Arm in the app at the final mounting position.MT
TAM-002Arming shall record a baseline: tilt (from the accelerometer), heading (from the compass, if fitted), a low-resolution reference image of the view, the Wi-Fi network fingerprint, and the position (if the board has satellite positioning).MT
TAM-003Mounting mode shall end after 60 minutes if the owner has not armed the camera. The camera then sleeps and sends only heartbeats until armed.MT

Detection

IDRequirementPriVer
TAM-010Moved. An accelerometer interrupt shall wake the camera. Movement lasting 2 seconds or more, or a tilt change of 8° or more from the baseline (both server-set), shall count as tamper.MT
TAM-011Turned. A heading change of 15° or more from the baseline shall count as tamper. A tilt sensor alone cannot see a pure left-right turn, so this needs a compass.ST
TAM-012View changed or covered. After any motion interrupt, and at every check-in, the camera shall send a small grayscale frame. The server shall compare it with the baseline and raise tamper when the view differs beyond a threshold, or stays dark or blocked for 5 minutes or more.MT
TAM-013Removed or opened. A magnet-and-reed switch shall detect the cover separating from the back plate and raise tamper.MT
TAM-014Knocks. A shock below the tamper level shall be logged with a photo. Three or more within 10 minutes shall send a warning without locking.MT
TAM-015Gone quiet. An armed camera shall check in every 15 minutes on Wi-Fi, or every 60 minutes on 4G only. The server shall raise "offline while armed" after two missed check-ins.MT
TAM-016Relocated. If the baseline Wi-Fi network is no longer seen, or the position moved more than 50 m, the camera shall report tamper.ST

Response

IDRequirementPriVer
TAM-020On tamper the camera shall first take a photo, even with no face in view, then send a tamper alert with the photo, the type, the time, the battery level and the position if known. It shall use the best link available and retry until delivered.MT
TAM-021Next the camera shall lock: stop all recognition events, turn off the light, drop the customer Wi-Fi and data session, and sleep. It may wake only to send beacons allowed by TAM-024.MT
TAM-022The lock shall be stored in the camera's flash and on the server. A reboot, power loss or Wi-Fi reset shall not clear it.MT
TAM-023The server shall refuse every request from a locked camera except tamper beacons and unlock checks.MT
TAM-024If the owner turned on recovery mode, a locked camera shall send a minimal beacon each hour (battery, signal, position if known) until the battery is low. Recovery mode is off by default.ST
TAM-025The owner shall be alerted at once on every enabled channel with high severity. The alert shall repeat after 15 minutes until the owner acknowledges it.MT

Unlock, service and ownership

IDRequirementPriVer
TAM-030Only the signed-in owner shall be able to unlock a camera, after a second confirmation (a fresh one-time code).MT
TAM-031An unlocked camera shall return to mounting mode and must be armed again, which records a new baseline.MT
TAM-032The owner shall be able to open a service window of 15 to 120 minutes (cleaning, battery swap) in which tamper is ignored. The owner is alerted when it starts and ends. It ends on its own.MT
TAM-033The owner shall be able to mark a camera stolen. Its key is then revoked for good.MT
TAM-034The owner shall be able to release a camera for a new owner. It becomes unclaimed with a new claim code.MT
TAM-035Staff shall unlock a camera only on the owner's verified request. The request and the action are written to the audit log.MI

Resistance to bypass

IDRequirementPriVer
TAM-040The tamper state, baseline and thresholds shall be controlled by the server. The camera applies them, and the server also checks the view and the check-ins itself.MT
TAM-041Pressing the reset button or opening the Wi-Fi hotspot while armed shall count as tamper.MT
TAM-042A locked or stolen camera shall be of no use to a thief for reaching customer data. It holds none, and its key no longer works.MI
TAM-043Secure boot and flash encryption, so a thief cannot re-flash the board and remove the lock logic.LI

What tamper detection cannot do

It cannot stop a theft. It raises the alert, takes a photo and makes the camera useless to the thief. Someone who jams Wi-Fi and 4G at the same time can delay the alert. The camera keeps trying, and the server raises "offline while armed" when check-ins stop. A thief can still re-use the bare hardware for their own purposes until secure boot is enabled. Wind, birds and slamming gates can cause false alarms, so the thresholds must be tuned in the pilot.

3.8 Architecture now, Platform product later (ARCH, BYO)

The second product sells the software alone. A customer adds any camera they own, sets up streaming, and uses Solavis recognition, alerts, plans and apps. The Camera MVP must be built so this needs new components, not a rewrite. The ARCH items below are MVP work. The BYO items are later.

Design now (MVP)

IDRequirementPriVer
ARCH-001The recognition service shall accept crops from any source type through one input contract: image, source ID, time, optional quality hints. It shall not depend on how the crop was produced.MI
ARCH-002The data model shall describe every camera as a source with a type (solavis_cam now; agent_rtsp, agent_onvif later) and a list of capabilities (for example pir, imu, reed, night_light, ota).MI
ARCH-003Plans shall limit sources by count and by allowed type.MT
ARCH-004Usage (events and bytes) shall be counted per source per month, so per-channel billing can be added without a schema change.MT
ARCH-005Apps and the API shall show only the features a source's capabilities support (for example tamper sensors appear only on Solavis cameras).SD
ARCH-006The recognition code shall live in a separate library with no web or device code in it, so the same code can run in the cloud and, later, in an on-site agent.MI
ARCH-007The device API shall be versioned (/v1) and shall be the only way any source talks to the cloud. No source type shall get a private path to the database or storage.MI

Platform product (later)

IDRequirementPriVer
BYO-001A customer shall add a third-party camera by RTSP address or by ONVIF discovery, through an on-site agent.LD
BYO-002The agent shall run on Linux, Windows and Docker, including a Raspberry Pi or a small PC. It shall need no inbound network port.LT
BYO-003The agent shall read the stream locally, find faces on the local machine, and send only face crops through the same device API as a Solavis camera.LT
BYO-004The agent shall register with a one-time code and then use its own key, which can be revoked.LT
BYO-005The agent shall report per-camera status in its heartbeat: stream up or down, frame rate, resolution, last frame time.LT
BYO-006The agent shall update itself with signed updates and report its version.LT
BYO-007One agent on a small PC shall handle at least 8 streams. Target, to be benchmarked.LT
BYO-008Plans and billing shall work per channel (per camera source) as well as per account.LT
BYO-009Third-party sources shall get the view-changed and gone-quiet checks (TAM-012, TAM-015). Motion and removal sensors do not apply.LT
BYO-010Camera passwords for third-party cameras shall be stored only on the agent's machine and never sent to the cloud.LI
BYO-011The product shall publish a list of tested camera models and stream formats (H.264 and H.265 over RTSP, ONVIF Profile S or T).LI
BYO-012The cloud shall not pull streams from customer networks. Customers shall not have to open ports.LI
BYO-013A self-hosted edition with a licence server may be offered to customers who will not use the cloud.LD

Why an on-site agent

Pulling video from a customer's home or shop network into our cloud would need open ports, large video bandwidth, and storage of far more footage. An agent on the customer's side does the heavy work locally and sends only small face crops, the same thing a Solavis camera sends. The cloud stays the same for both products.

Pilot acceptance

The MVP is accepted when every M requirement passes its verification, with these pilot results: recognition correct on at least 90% of the test set, at most 1 false watch list alert per camera per week, no dead battery in the pilot, and at least 3 of 5 pilot users agree to pay.
