How Solavis works

From someone walking up to an alert on your phone, in a few seconds.

  1. It wakes

    A motion sensor wakes the camera. The rest of the time it sleeps to save power.

  2. It looks

    The camera takes a photo and finds the face. A white light turns on at night.

  3. It checks

    A small face image is sent over an encrypted connection to your Solavis account and compared with the people you registered.

  4. It tells you

    You get the photo and a result on your phone: known, unknown, or a possible match to your watch list.

What you can do

  • Register your people with their consent, in the app, in under a minute each.
  • Keep a watch list of people you do not want at your gate. Every match is shown as "possible match" for you to review.
  • Get alerts in the Solavis app or on Telegram.
  • Review the history with photos, times and results, for as many days as your plan includes.
  • Stay up to date. The camera receives signed software updates over the air.
  • Stay in control. If a camera is lost or stolen, you can switch it off from your account.

Your data stays under control

A security camera should not create a new risk. Solavis is designed so that the camera holds nothing worth stealing.

Nothing stored on the camera

The camera keeps no face data and no storage passwords. A stolen camera can be blocked from your account.

Separate and encrypted

Each account is kept apart from every other. Data travels over encrypted connections and is encrypted when stored.

You decide what is kept

Photos are removed after the number of days in your plan. You can ask for an export or a full deletion of your data.

Used responsibly

You decide who is on your lists and you are asked to confirm that the people you register agree. We provide a sign for your gate that tells visitors a face recognition camera is in use. A match is always a "possible match" for you to review, never proof. Solavis is not a door lock and must not be used to open a door by itself.

Questions

What happens when the Wi-Fi goes down?

The 4G model switches to its mobile SIM automatically and returns to Wi-Fi when it is back. If neither is available, the camera keeps recent events and sends them as soon as it reconnects.

Does it work at night?

A white light turns on when someone approaches in the dark, which gives the camera a clear face to look at. We test accuracy in daylight, dusk and backlight during the pilot.

What about cloudy weeks and the rainy season?

The camera sleeps most of the time, so it uses very little power. Its battery is designed to last about ten days without sun. We are measuring this in real conditions during the pilot.

Do I need a SIM card?

No, the Lite model uses Wi-Fi only. The 4G model has a SIM slot for backup. Whether the SIM is included or supplied by you will be confirmed at launch.

Can it unlock my gate?

No. Solavis tells you who is there. It does not open doors, because face recognition from a camera is not secure enough to be a lock.

Where is my data stored?

On Solavis servers, in your own separated account. The camera itself holds no photos or faces after it sends them.

What if my camera is stolen?

Switch the camera off in your account. It stops sending and cannot be used on another account.
