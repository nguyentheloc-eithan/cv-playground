1. Scope and hardware requirements

This document defines the Solavis Camera hardware for the MVP: parts, wiring, power, enclosure, assembly and test. It uses ready-made boards, with no custom circuit board. Requirements come from SOL-SRS-001. The Platform (bring-your-own camera) product has no Solavis hardware, so it is not covered here.

4. Electrical design

4.1 Power chain

  1. Panel plus goes to the charger input through a Schottky diode. Panel minus to ground.
  2. The charger output feeds the cell and the board's battery input. The cell has its own protection, or a protection board sits between cell and load.
  3. A thermistor (NTC) taped to the cell goes to the charger's temperature pin so charging stops outside 0 to 45 °C.
  4. The board runs from the cell through its own regulator. The modem takes peaks of about 2 A, so add a 470 µF or larger capacitor close to the modem supply.

Pin names and the regulator arrangement depend on the exact board. Fill in the pin map in section 4.3 once the board is in hand.

4.4 Tamper sensors

  • Accelerometer is fixed rigidly to the board (double-sided tape is not enough, use screws or glue) and the board is fixed to the cover. It runs in a low-power mode and wakes the board on activity above a threshold. A tilt reading comes from the gravity direction.
  • Compass reads heading. Metal gates and wires disturb it, so only a change from the baseline is used, never an absolute direction.
  • Reed switch sits in the cover and the magnet in the back plate, 8 to 12 mm apart when the cover is on. A strong magnet held outside can defeat it, which is why it is one layer of several (SOL-SRS-001 section 3.7).
  • Image check needs no extra part. The camera sends a small grayscale frame, and the server compares it with the one taken at arming.

5. Mechanical design

A wedge-shaped box, 125 wide × 90 deep × 79 high mm, 55 mm high at the front. The solar panel sits in a pocket on the 15° sloped top. Camera, motion sensor and light are on the front. The back plate screws to the post or wall, and the cover slides onto it, held by 3 screws from underneath. Source files are in hardware/enclosure/ and build_enclosure.py rebuilds them. Status: Rev B, not printed yet.

6. Thermal and environment

  • A closed box in the Vietnamese sun can pass 60 °C inside. Use a light colour, keep the box in shade where the site allows, and let the panel shade the top.
  • Fit a vent plug if tests show condensation. Add a silica gel pack.
  • Place the cell away from the board and out of direct sunlight. Charging stops above 45 °C by design.
  • Use stainless screws and coat exposed board edges with conformal spray.
  • Rain: no rating is claimed. The goal is splash-proof (IPX4), proven only by the test in section 10.

7. Assembly procedure

  1. Press the 3 inserts into the shelves of the back plate with a soldering iron. Let cool.
  2. Glue the magnet into its pocket (Rev C) with the correct pole facing the reed holder. Mark the pole.
  3. Glue the window disc into the cover recess with silicone. Glue the hood around it. Fit the PIR and the light and seal them.
  4. Fit the solar panel into its pocket with silicone. Pass its cable through the hole and seal the hole.
  5. Mount the main board on its posts. Connect the camera, accelerometer, compass, reed, light and battery sense.
  6. Fit the cell in its holder inside the cradle, with the NTC taped to the cell.
  7. Run the provisioning and end-of-line test (sections 8 and 9) before closing.
  8. Fit the foam tape on the cover rim, slide the cover onto the plate, fit 3 screws from underneath.
  9. Apply the serial and QR label inside the cover, and a second copy in the packing.

8. Provisioning and labels

  • A provisioning script flashes the firmware, creates the device ID and key, writes them to protected storage, registers the camera in the server inventory with a claim code, and prints the QR label.
  • The label shows the serial, the QR claim code, the model (Lite or 4G) and the firmware version.
  • The claim code is shown only on the label and in the box. It is not stored on the camera.
  • Target: 3 minutes of machine time per camera, under 30 minutes per assembled unit.

11. Regulatory and safety

  • Wi-Fi and 4G radios need Vietnamese type approval for the finished product before public sale. Pilot with known users only.
  • Use protected cells from a known supplier, and ship by ground. Lithium cells have air freight limits.
  • Fuse the battery lead. Keep the cell out of direct sun and away from the board.
  • The label shall carry the model, serial, and the contact details of the seller.

13. Custom board, later

After the pilot, one custom board can replace the boards and modules. Its goals: 200 µA sleep, one board with modem, camera connector, sensors and charger, the same enclosure footprint, and a smaller, cheaper box. It also allows a hardware cell-removal alarm with a small backup capacitor to send a last alert. It is outside this document.
